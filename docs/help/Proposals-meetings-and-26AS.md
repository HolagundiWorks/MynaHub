# Fee proposals, meetings, attendance and the 26AS check

## Fee proposals (Practice > Fee proposals)
A proposal is what the practice offers to charge a client. Pick how the fee is worked out and fill in only the figures for that choice:
- **A percentage of the cost of the work:** the estimated cost and the percentage.
- **A rate per unit of area:** the built-up area and the rate for each unit.
- **A lump sum:** the fee itself.

When the project is shared with Falcon through the company file, the proposal can show the latest Falcon estimate total and **Use Falcon estimate as cost** (read-only from CoreOp's saved estimate). Running-account (RA) bill totals from Falcon appear beside the same project for fee and invoice screens.

GST is added on top and the total is shown on the list. A proposal starts as a draft. **Mark sent** when it goes to the client, then **Client decided** and choose Accepted or Declined. Once a client has decided, the proposal cannot change; make a new one. A sent proposal shows "(lapsed)" after its valid-until date. A proposal does not post to the books.

## Meetings and decisions (Practice > Meetings and decisions)
Record each meeting with who was there, what was discussed and what was decided. Give a follow-up date if something must be done. The follow-up shows "(due)" from its date until you set **Follow-up done** to Yes.

## Attendance (Office > Attendance)
Choose a day and mark each person present, half day, absent, on leave or holiday, then **Save the day**. Marking a day again replaces the earlier mark. A person can be marked only for days between joining and leaving, and a day that has not come cannot be marked. The month table counts days present, half days, absent, leave and holidays, and the days worked with half days counted as halves.

## 26AS check (Reports and tax > 26AS check)
Form 26AS lists the tax your clients' deductors say they deducted from payments to you. Save the statement from TRACES as a CSV and choose **Import a 26AS CSV**. It needs columns for the deductor's name, the deductor's TAN, the date, the amount paid and the tax deducted; a section column is used if there is one. Importing the same statement twice is safe, and a file with any bad line is refused whole, with the line numbers.

**Check** then compares a period with the TDS you booked on receipts. A deduction is matched when the client's TAN and the tax amount agree and the dates are within 120 days. You see what matched, what the deductor reported that you have not booked, and what you booked that is not reported, including the clients that have no TAN on file. Nothing is changed in the books.
